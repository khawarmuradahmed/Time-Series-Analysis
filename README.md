##Executive Summary

This analysis evaluates the efficacy of the FRED Retail Sales Index as a leading indicator for Walmart’s quarterly revenue. By implementing a SARIMAX framework, I achieved a Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) of 2.1%, representing a 2.6x improvement over the 5.44% baseline provided by a seasonal drift model. Notably, the model identified a statistically significant inverse relationship (p = 0.011) between the macro-economic index and Walmart’s performance, reinforcing the company's role as a defensive retail staple.
Method & Results

To establish a rigorous performance benchmark, I implemented the sktime NaiveForecaster using two distinct strategies: Last and Drift. The "Last" strategy assumes pure seasonal persistence (e.g., using December 2024 to predict December 2025), while the "Drift" strategy focuses on preserving the long-term historical trend. In this analysis, the Drift and Last models yielded Mean Absolute Percentage Errors (MAPE) of 5.44% and 12.75%, respectively. Consequently, the 5.44% MAPE from the Drift strategy was established as the baseline.

With 5.44% as the baseline, my goal was to determine whether the retail-sales index could be a good leading indicator for the revenue generated by Walmart on a quarterly basis. Initial observations suggested that the retail-sales index could inform the model of some extenuating circumstances that could disrupt the economy (such as a pandemic, geopolitical conflict etc). However, it must be noted that the retail-sales index might not be able to account for the resilience that a global company like Walmart has. A simple fact like that is demonstrated in 2020, in which the retail-sales index fell while Walmart’s revenue did not take that much of a hit.

With all of that in mind, I used SARIMAX (Seasonal Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average with Exogenous Regressors) to create an interpretable linear model that takes into account seasonal trends, previous values, as well as exogenous regressors (which in our case is retail-sales index). The specific model I used takes into account the general momentum, and change in revenue from month to month. This significantly outperformed the baseline by 2.6x with a MAPE of 2.1%. Interestingly, the coefficient was -5.674e-05 and significant (p < 0.05). After scaling, a one-unit increase in the FRED Retail Sales Index is associated with a $56,740 decrease in Walmart's quarterly revenue.
Discussion

Because of the counterintuitive results, further scrutiny is warranted. Even though the retail-sales index is significant, the magnitude of its impact is up for debate. The index and Walmart’s revenue generally trended together until 2020. As a result, I am assuming that the post-COVID data is having an outsized impact on the model. While the inverse relationship sounds counterintuitive on the surface, there is some rationale behind this. For example, Walmart tends to be one of the cheaper grocery stores, and so when the economy contracts consumers that would go to other stores like Harmons or Trader Joe’s might opt for Walmart
